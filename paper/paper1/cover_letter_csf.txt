We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Structural Reconfiguration of Systemic Risk in Financial Markets: An Empirical Network Analysis" for consideration in Chaos, Solitons & Fractals.

(1) Significance of the advance
This work introduces a reproducible rolling framework that jointly tracks hierarchical correlation structure, planar financial-network topology, and Forman-Ricci curvature in the Brazilian equity market (B3) over 360 windows spanning 1995-2025. The manuscript contributes by integrating four elements into a single protocol: extraction of a planar backbone from full distance matrices, joint hierarchical and topological diagnostics, size-adjusted residual comparisons between crisis and non-crisis regimes, and curvature-informed assessment of bridge fragility. The result is an empirical taxonomy of topological statistics by regime role. Empirically, we document a coherent multi-scale signature of stress: mean planar distance falls from 1.1616 (non-crisis) to 1.0887 (crisis), while Ward residual means shift from 0.1453 to -0.2942, with concurrent centrality concentration and curvature deterioration on bridge edges. This integrated structural characterization, validated with Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Marchenko-Pastur diagnostics, advances prior studies that treat these layers in isolation.

The manuscript is positioned as an exploratory structural monitoring and complex systems contribution. Its value is to provide interpretable and reproducible diagnostics of systemic regime transitions, with direct relevance for network-based financial surveillance.

(2) Suitability for Chaos, Solitons & Fractals
The manuscript centers on nonlinear structural dynamics, complex network geometry, and regime transitions in financial systems. It engages directly with prior CSF contributions on systemic importance, diversification effects, network fragility, and multilayer financial dynamics, positioning the contribution squarely within the journal scope.
